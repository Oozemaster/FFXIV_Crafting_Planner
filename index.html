FFXIV Weekly Craft Planner

Splits the week's best crafts into claimable bundles. Built for weekend restocking.

Setup

Start by loading recipes. Takes about half a minute.

What to consider crafting

Housing and furnishing categories are pre-selected — slow-moving and high-value, which suits a once-a-week restock.

What this does and does not know

Prices come from what actually sold, not what people are asking. Listings far above the real sale price are ignored and not counted as competition.

You cannot sell more than you made. Expected sales are capped at the number of units in your bundle.

An item with no sale history is dropped however lucrative it looks.

One listing holds one item. True for furnishings, false for stackable materials, which are undervalued here.

Unspoiled and timed nodes are not detected automatically. The game data links node contents inconsistently and the flag could not be trusted. Instead there is a hand-kept list near the top of this file's code, marked MANUAL NOTES — anything added there gets tagged wherever it appears and pulled to the top of the material lists. Company Seal materials are still detected automatically, and those are reliable.

Rivals keep listing after you do. The competition is not only what is on the board today — over the week other crafters add more, and in a settled market they add about as many as sell, because every unit sold was listed by somebody. Comparing your stock against a snapshot while demand is a weekly rate was mixing the two and flattered every recommendation. "Rival restocking %" controls this: 100 assumes a settled market, 0 assumes nobody else lists all week. Lower it if a week of real results shows things selling better than predicted.

The market board never runs out. Your own world's listings are used first, cheapest first. When they run short, the remainder is priced at the same average on the assumption you travel to another world to buy them — which you can, anywhere in North America. Crafts needing that trip are tagged.

Gathered materials are not free. Everything is valued at its market price, because anything you dig up you could have sold instead. Gathering versus buying decides whether you spend an evening or spend gil — it does not change what the item cost to make. An expensive material is expensive because it is hard to get, and that difficulty is a real cost however you pay it.

Progress does not save automatically from a file on your hard drive — the browser forbids it. Use Save and Load session, or host the page.
